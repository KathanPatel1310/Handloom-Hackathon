# Data Dictionary — Handloom Demand Forecasting Dataset

Every column below is tagged so the methodology can be defended to judges:

- **REAL** — actual named real-world entities (cluster names, states, specialties)
- **REAL-PROXY** — a real, freely available public signal used as an input
- **SYNTHETIC-CALIBRATED** — generated by a documented mechanism grounded in real
  patterns (seasonality, festival timing, price elasticity), not random noise
- **SYNTHETIC-PLACEHOLDER** — a stand-in series that MUST be replaced with a real
  source before final submission if time allows; the script tells you exactly
  where to plug the real series in

## demand_weekly.csv — CORE MODELING TABLE (synthetic, mechanistically generated)
`units_ordered` = base_level(cluster,product) × seasonal × festival_multiplier ×
wedding_multiplier × cotton_price_elasticity × cluster_trend + Poisson noise.
Every coefficient is a named constant at the top of `generate_dataset.py` with
a one-line justification — nothing is a buried magic number.

Validation (printed by the script, re-check after any edits):
- Demand lift near major festivals vs normal weeks: **~51%**
- Correlation of total weekly demand with google_trends_index: **0.82**

## orders_log.csv — order-level disaggregation
Directly answers the two parts of the problem statement most teams will skip:
**"lack of visibility into future orders"** (promised vs actual delivery dates)
and **"delayed payments"** (payment_due_date vs payment_received_date). In this
run, average payment delay vs due date is **9 days**, with **60%** of orders
paid at least somewhat late — a realistic mix, not "everyone is late."
